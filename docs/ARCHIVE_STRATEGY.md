# Archive Strategy

The Memory of Humanity treats archives as sources, not as a single database to be copied wholesale.

## Source classes

The protocol can represent newspapers, periodicals, books, manuals, photographs, maps, government records, oral archives and other documentary sources.

## Collection registry

An archive collection records provider, region, source types, date coverage, discovery/API URLs, OCR availability, bulk-data availability, access model and rights notes.

## Newspaper strategy

Newspapers preserve time-specific reporting, advertisements, public notices, language, opinion and community perspectives. They remain attributable to publication, issue, date and archive.

The project should prefer public-domain/openly licensed material, official APIs/datasets/metadata exports, links and citations for restricted collections, and lawful user-provided copies where permitted.

Digitisation does not automatically make a work public domain. Copyright, database rights, contractual access terms, privacy, cultural rights and jurisdiction-specific restrictions can still apply.

## Initial discovery targets

The registry can include international and regional collections such as Europeana Newspapers, Library of Congress / Chronicling America, Trove / National Library of Australia, South African newspaper and cultural archives, and government gazettes.

These are discovery targets, not claims that all material is freely reusable.

## Preservation pipeline

catalogue -> discover -> acquire lawful representation -> preserve checksum -> OCR/transcription -> extract entities -> link memories -> retain source provenance

OCR is a derived representation. The original scan or page remains the source record where available.

## The archive is a witness

A newspaper is not automatically “the truth.” It is evidence of what a publication recorded, published or circulated at a particular time. Multiple publications may disagree. Those disagreements are historically meaningful records.
